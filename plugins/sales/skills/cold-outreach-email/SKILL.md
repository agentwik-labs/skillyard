# Cold Outreach Email

A cold email is read in about five seconds on a phone by someone who did not ask for it. It earns a reply only if it is obviously about the reader, short, and asks for something small. Write for that reader, not for the sender's pride in the product.

## Workflow

1. **Get the inputs.** What is sold, to whom (role and company type), the one outcome customers get, proof (a customer, a number), and the sender's name and role. If any are missing, ask; do not invent proof.
2. **Find the reason for this person, now.** One specific, verifiable observation: a job posting, a launch, a stated priority, a tool they use, a post they wrote. If there is none, say the email will be generic and the reply rate will be low.
3. **Pick one problem** the role likely owns that the offer solves. One. Lists of benefits read as spam.
4. **Draft the first email** in the structure below, 50-90 words.
5. **Write 2-3 follow-ups**, each adding something new (a proof point, a useful resource, a different angle), spaced 3, 5 and 7 business days apart. The last one is a polite close-out.
6. **Write 2 subject lines** per email: 2-5 words, lowercase is fine, no clickbait.
7. **Run the checklist** before handing over.

## Structure of the first email

1. Line 1: the observation about them (not about you).
2. Line 2: the problem it suggests, as a question or a plain statement.
3. Line 3: what you do about it, with one proof point (customer or number).
4. Line 4: a small ask ("Worth a 15-minute look next week?" or "Want me to send the 1-page teardown?").

## Rules

- **Under 90 words.** Longer emails get skimmed, then archived.
- **One ask, easy to say yes to.** Asking for 30 minutes from a stranger is big; offering a specific useful thing is small.
- **"You" before "we".** Count the pronouns; the reader should appear more than the sender.
- **No fake familiarity.** No "Hope you're well", "Quick question", "Just following up", or invented mutual connections. They read as automation and erode trust.
- **Proof must be real.** Only cite customers and numbers the user confirms; made-up results are a legal and reputational risk.
- **Plain text, no images, at most one link,** no attachments in the first touch. Links and images hurt deliverability.
- **Respect opt-outs and local rules.** Include a simple way to opt out and flag for a professional if the user is unsure about consent rules (e.g. CAN-SPAM, GDPR, CASL) in their market.
- **Personalize at scale with one variable line,** not mail-merge fluff like {{company}} dropped into a generic sentence.

## Checklist

- [ ] Opens with something true about the reader
- [ ] One problem, one proof, one ask
- [ ] 50-90 words, readable on a phone without scrolling
- [ ] No buzzwords: "synergy", "leverage", "revolutionary", "best-in-class"
- [ ] Subject line under 6 words, not misleading
- [ ] Each follow-up adds new value

## Example

Bad:
> Subject: Revolutionize your operations!
> Hi Sam, I hope this email finds you well! I'm reaching out because Acme is the leading AI-powered platform that helps companies of all sizes streamline workflows, boost productivity and cut costs. We'd love to schedule a 30-minute demo to show you everything we can do...

Good:
> Subject: 3 ops hires in a month
> Sam, saw Brightline is hiring three order-ops coordinators. Usually that means order volume outgrew the email-to-ERP process. We automate that step; Dalton Supply cut re-keying from 15 hours a week to 2. Want the 1-page breakdown of how they set it up?
> Jordan, Founder at Acme

## Output

```
### Email 1 (day 0)
Subject options: <a> / <b>
<body, 50-90 words>

### Email 2 (day 3)
Subject: Re: <thread>
<body, under 70 words, new proof or resource>

### Email 3 (day 8)
<body, under 60 words, new angle>

### Email 4 (day 15, close-out)
<body, under 40 words>

### Notes
- Personalization source: <what was observed and where>
- Assumptions to confirm: <proof points, names>
```
